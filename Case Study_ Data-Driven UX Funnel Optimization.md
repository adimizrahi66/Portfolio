# **Data-Driven UX: Transforming Affiliate Funnels Through Iterative Performance Optimization**

**My Role:** Senior Product Designer  |  **Team:** 1 Product Manager, 2 Data Analysts, 1 Frontend Engineer  |  **Duration:** Ongoing Optimization (4 months)

## ---

**Overview**

At Roundforest, we run high-volume Google traffic campaigns that send millions of global shoppers (primarily from the US) to data-driven "Top 10" product review lists on our main site, [BestReviews.Guide](https://www.bestreviews.guide/laptops). The platform operates on an affiliate model, earning a commission from Amazon whenever a user clicks through our recommendations and makes a purchase.

In a high-velocity traffic ecosystem like this, even tiny friction points in the user journey can lead to massive revenue drops. This case study highlights how we ditched guesswork for fast A/B testing—completely transforming the funnel to make product discovery seamless for users and optimizing bottom-of-funnel mobile conversion triggers.

Challenge → We observed two core issues:

* Users were moving through content quickly with **low meaningful engagement**  
* The experience lacked clear guidance for fast, confident decision-making—especially on mobile

We also needed more reliable **short-cycle UX signals** to support rapid experimentation.

## **Strategy →** 

I led an experimentation-driven UX process focused on improving early behavioral signals and reducing decision friction. Instead of large redesigns, I broke the experience into small, testable hypotheses around intent capture, clarity, and scanability. Each iteration was validated through A/B testing, with a focus on interaction quality and decision-flow improvements rather than assumptions.

We broke the conversion funnel into two main execution phases:

* **Phase 1:** The "Narrow Down" Filtering Funnel (Mid-Funnel Intent Capture)  
* **Phase 2:** The Mobile Product Page Redesign (Bottom-Funnel Conversion Push)  
* **Phase 3:** Feature-Rich Layout Experiment (Did Not Scale)

Results → Through continuous iteration and testing, the updated experiences led to:

* Significant improvements in user engagement across devices  
* Consistent increases in interaction with key decision elements  
* Noticeable uplift in downstream conversion-related behaviors

These gains compounded over time, demonstrating the impact of small, targeted UX improvements in high-traffic environments.

## **Phase 1: The "Narrow Down" Interactive Filter Funnel** 

### **How do we help high-intent traffic make faster purchasing choices?**

When users search for product reviews, they arrive with high purchase intent but low patience. Our initial baseline design presented comprehensive product lists, but users struggled to find specific matches quickly. We introduced the "Narrow Down" feature—interactive tag chips designed to let users instantly segment products.  
`[ Paid Ad Click ] ➔ [ Choice Paralysis (Old Baseline) ] ➔ Drop-off`  
`[ Paid Ad Click ] ➔ [ Interactive "Narrow Down" Chips ] ➔ Clear Intent ➔ Higher Clickout`

### **Iteration 1: Establishing the Intent Baseline**

* The first iteration focused on validating user interest in the filtering concept and refining early copy and interaction patterns. We ran an A/B test comparing two variants against the existing baseline to understand whether users would engage with the new filtering direction at all.  
* **The Data:** One variant showed a clear preference from users, with improved interaction rates across both desktop and mobile. However, overall engagement levels were still not strong enough to justify a broader rollout or meaningful change to the baseline experience.  
* **UX Takeaway:** The results indicated that user demand for filtering existed, but the feature was not yet sufficiently visible or intuitive. This highlighted the importance of improving discoverability and strengthening the visual hierarchy of interactive elements.

### **Iteration 2: Visual Prominence & Layout Optimization**

To improve discoverability and reduce banner blindness, I redesigned the interaction pattern to increase visual prominence while keeping the experience non-intrusive.

**Visual Hierarchy:** I improved the contrast and clarity of the filter chips to make them more clearly identifiable as interactive elements.

**Layout Behavior:** I adjusted the filtering module to remain accessible during scroll, ensuring users could engage with it throughout their journey while maintaining overall page clarity.

\*image placement\*

**Outcome:** The changes led to a significant increase in user interaction across both desktop and mobile, validating the importance of visibility and persistent access in high-intent flows.

### **Iteration 3: Contextual Defaults & Behavior Guidance**

* Building on the previous iteration, I introduced a contextual default state to better guide user behavior and improve initial clarity of the filtering system.  
* **Design Decision:** A default filter state was pre-selected to immediately signal interactivity and help users understand the system’s purpose while providing a structured starting point for exploration.  
* **Outcome:** This change further improved engagement and reinforced the effectiveness of using guided defaults to reduce decision friction in high-intent flows.

### **Phase 2: Mobile Product Page Optimization**

* After validating mid-funnel filtering, I focused on improving the mobile product page experience where final clickout decisions occur. Given mobile users’ fast scanning behavior, the goal was to reduce cognitive load and improve added value for the user to improve decision-making.  
* I designed the product page to maintain visual and structural continuity with the list page, keeping key UI patterns consistent to create a seamless end-to-end flow. Since the purchase happens in an external marketplace, this continuity helped reduce cognitive friction and user confusion.

### **Phase 3: Feature-Rich Layout Experiment (Did Not Scale)**

* We tested a more feature-dense mobile product card design that introduced additional product specifications and interactive elements. The hypothesis was that richer information would support more confident decision-making, particularly for an older user base that benefited from more explicit detail.  
* The UI was intentionally designed with high contrast, clear borders, and strong separation between elements to improve readability and accessibility for less digital-native users.

* **Outcome:** The experiment did not improve performance and negatively impacted clickout behavior.

  \*image placement\* 

* **Insight:** While users engaged with the added interactive elements, the increased density disrupted page hierarchy and added friction for fast-scanning mobile traffic. This reinforced a key learning: for high-intent users, clarity and speed are more effective than feature richness.

1. ### **Key Takeaways**

2. **Design for proxy signals:** In fast-moving e-commerce environments, optimizing upstream behaviors like engagement and readability is often the most reliable way to improve downstream conversion outcomes.  
3. **Friction compounds at scale:** Small UX improvements become significant when applied across large traffic volumes, leading to meaningful impact on overall performance.  
4. **Mobile requires intentional simplification:** Effective mobile UX prioritizes clarity, reduced density, and interaction patterns that support quick scanning and decision-making.
