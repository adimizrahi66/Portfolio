Overview

Scaling creative production for Meta acquisition

Profitable Meta acquisition at Roundforest relies on testing a high daily volume of ad creatives. I led the UX/UI design for CopyCat – an internal, AI-driven platform – to solve a severe operational bottleneck. The team struggled with a fragmented, six-tool workflow while trying to hit their ROAS goals. The challenge was transforming this disconnected process into a single, automated ecosystem that could scale production.

Challenge →

Creative production was capped at 70–100 creatives per day, while breakeven required ~130 and profitable scale required 500+. A fragmented workflow across six different tools made scaling slow and inefficient.

Strategy →

I embedded with Roundforest’s Meta acquisition team for two weeks, mapped the end-to-end workflow, and redesigned it as one unified platform – consolidating tools, automating repetitive tasks, and keeping human review where it mattered.

Results →

Reduced content production time by 75%, consolidated 6 tools into 1, and eliminated 36 manual clicks through automation.

UX Strategy

A platform, not just a generator

I approached the system as a holistic platform, not just a creative generator. Three principles guided every decision:

Unify →

Bring all tools into one system, replacing six disconnected steps with a single continuous flow.

Automate →

Automate everything possible – data handling, layout, and asset generation – to remove manual work.

Review →

Keep human review where it matters, protecting quality on high-sensitivity decisions.

Key Decision

Choosing a guided wizard over one big screen

The biggest call in the project was how to structure article and creative creation. Keeping the familiar layout was the safe option. I chose to redesign it from scratch, knowing it carried a real risk.

Option A

Improve the existing screen

Keep the legacy all-in-one layout and refine it. The existing screen was cumbersome and crowded: every tool and setting was always on display, including features that weren’t relevant to the task at hand.

Upside: a smaller change, with no relearning for the current team.
Downside: polishing a cluttered layout keeps the clutter, and with no enforced order, steps stay easy to skip.
The legacy CopyCat screen
Option BChosen

A guided wizard with clear steps

Break creation into a fixed sequence, where each step unlocks the next and nothing can be skipped.

Upside: a predictable, systematic process that is easy to learn.
Risk: users would need time to relearn a tool they used every day.

Why I took the risk

The team changes often

The people producing articles and creatives rotated frequently. A tool that teaches its own process mattered more than familiarity for whoever was there today.

Order prevents errors

A fixed sequence keeps every task consistent and removes the human errors that came from skipped or out-of-order steps.

Short learning, long payoff

I expected the relearning period to be short, while the structure would keep paying off as volume grew.

Getting the PM and engineer on board

At first it was hard to convince the team that a change this big was worth it. What moved the conversation was separating the interface from the system underneath: the features and the existing technology would stay almost the same, and the redesign would not make the backend more complex. Most of the change lived in the front end.

To keep that promise, I built the new front end myself on top of the existing system the PM and engineer had built, so the redesign shipped without adding backend work.

Solution

One task list that drives the whole flow

The system is built around a task & content list – the starting point of the daily workflow. Each task includes article (landing page) generation and creative generation. The system pulls all relevant data directly from each task, creating one continuous flow from idea to live campaign.

Key Design Decisions

Single Source of Truth: Task rows automatically aggregate all required metadata to trigger article and creative creation without manual data entry.
Reduced Cognitive Load: Integrated AI tools minimize steps and clicks, accelerating the entire production workflow.
Intentional Review Gates: Avoided total automation to ensure quality control. Since creation costs money, explicit human approval is required before proceeding.
Clear Flow & Status: Completed tasks highlight in green to give immediate status feedback before handoff to PPC.
AI Review

AI drafts. People decide.

CopyCat uses GPT, Gemini and Ideogram to draft article text, banners and ad creatives. Their output varies in quality, and every generation costs money, so the interface treats AI as a first draft. The reviewer can always see what the AI wrote, fix one part without losing the rest, and nothing moves forward until a person saves it.

1

Label where content came from

Every field is tagged “auto-filled from task” or “AI generated”, so reviewers know exactly what needs checking.

2

Regenerate one field at a time

A weak description can be regenerated on its own, without throwing away a banner or copy that already works.

3

Offer options, not one answer

The sharing description comes as three variants, which turns reviewing into choosing.

4

Edit, swap or upload

All text stays editable. The banner can be regenerated from its visible prompt or replaced with an uploaded image.

5

A person makes the final call

Work autosaves as a draft, but only an explicit “Save Article” moves the task forward.

Reflection

What I’d do differently

The wizard was a bet, and the results backed it. Still, with a one-month timeline, it was validated mostly through real usage after launch. Next time I would explore more iterations of the article and creative flow before committing, and run deeper usability tests with the people who use it daily, to confirm earlier that this structure was the right one.
