Background

Profitable Meta acquisition runs on creative volume.

Roundforest wins on Meta by testing many ad creatives every day. I led UX and UI for CopyCat, the internal AI platform that turns a product task into a landing-page article and a set of ad creatives.

Problem

A faster process that became harder to run.

A no-code system had already lifted output by 30%. But it lacked context and didn't fit the team's workflow, so they stitched together six tools around it. Production got faster and much more complex.

Research

Two weeks inside the Meta acquisition team.

I joined the team for two weeks to experience the workflow from the inside: the real pain points, the constraints, and the gaps between their tools and what they actually needed.

Mapping

Mapping the workflow showed where it broke.

I mapped everything from ideation to campaign launch. It surfaced redundant steps, tool fragmentation, and clear room to consolidate and automate.

Ideation→Article→Creatives→Campaign→Testing

Unify

Replace six disconnected steps with one continuous flow.

Automate

Review

Keep people in charge where quality and cost are at stake.

Decision

I chose a guided wizard over one big screen.

The biggest call was how to structure creation. Keeping the familiar layout was safe. I chose to redesign it, knowing it carried a real risk.

Option A

Improve the existing screen

Every tool and setting always on display, with no enforced order.

Keeps the clutter, and steps stay easy to skip.

Option B Chosen

A guided wizard

A fixed sequence where each step unlocks the next.

Risk: the team relearns a tool they use daily.

The five steps

Setup → Templates → Generate → Review & select → Download

1 / 5

The team changes often

A tool that teaches its own process beats familiarity.

Order prevents errors

No more skipped or out-of-order steps.

Short learning, long payoff

The structure keeps paying off as volume grows.

To get the PM and engineer on board, I built the new front end myself on the existing system, so the redesign added no backend work.

Solution

One task list drives the whole flow.

Every day starts from the task list. Each task carries the data needed to generate its article and its creatives, so nobody re-types anything between steps.

Generate article→Generate creatives→Review & approve

Single source of truth

Task rows hold all metadata, so creation needs no manual entry.

Intentional review gates

Generation costs money, so a person approves before anything moves on.

Clear status

Finished tasks turn green before handoff to PPC.

Dashboard tab

Throughput and bottlenecks by owner and category, with no spreadsheet export.

The whole flow in 30 seconds

From a task row to an article, through the five creative steps, and back to the list.

Deep dive

Dashboard

Completed and open tasks by owner, section and category. The team sees throughput and bottlenecks without exporting to a spreadsheet.

Article generator

AI drafts the landing-page article from the task's page title. Every field shows where it came from and can be regenerated on its own.

Templates

Templates matched to the product category come pre-selected. Uncheck what you don't want, then generate everything in one run.

Review & select

Each creative is tagged Good, Needs review, or with a specific issue. Auto-pick the best, confirm the checklist, and only then download.

AI review

CopyCat uses GPT, Gemini and Ideogram for article text, banners and ads. Quality varies and every generation costs money, so the interface treats AI output as a first draft.

Learned

Separate the interface from the system. Test the bet earlier.

Make big changes cheap for the team. The wizard was a hard sell until I showed the features and backend would stay the same, and then built the front end myself to keep that promise.

Validate before committing. With one month, the wizard was proven mostly by real use after launch. Next time I'd explore more iterations and run usability tests with daily users first.
