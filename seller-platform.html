Overview

Overview

Roundforest collaborates directly with Amazon sellers under a performance-based model. When sellers offer a higher commission rate than Amazon's default, Roundforest leverages its performance marketing capabilities to promote their products and drive sales.

Seller Platform is a dedicated management system that enables these sellers to monitor their sales performance and manage inventory for all products promoted through Roundforest.

Challenge →

Integrate active sellers via their Amazon Creator accounts – motivating long-time sellers to migrate existing campaigns while encouraging adoption of higher-value configuration options, without forcing the choice.

Strategy →

Eliminate friction at every step. Automate what we can, minimize required actions, and design a low-effort path that's optimized for completion over compliance.

Results →

A majority of sellers completed the connection process in the first week of launch, and a measurable portion adopted higher-value configuration options – boosting engagement and revenue.

Defining the Problem

How do we encourage sellers to connect their Amazon Creator accounts?

No Clear Incentive: Sellers saw the connection as an extra administrative burden with no immediate, obvious value.
High Cognitive Load: Already overwhelmed by managing complex Amazon inventories, sellers were highly likely to drop off if faced with a tedious process.
Risk for High-Volume Sellers: The friction was greatest for large-scale sellers managing hundreds of items, making them the most likely to ignore the update entirely.
Strategy & Exploration

User Flow Mapping

To address the adoption barrier, I first mapped out the best way to notify sellers about the migration without disrupting their daily workflow. I explored three approaches:

Option 1
Forced Onboarding

Blocking the login flow until the connection was completed.

Risk: High friction and immediate user frustration.
Option 2
Persistent Banners

Displaying a modal upon login, followed by a persistent global header banner.

Risk: Banner blindness or visual clutter.
Option 3
Chosen
Contextual Discovery

Keeping the core workflow intact and using a targeted sidebar tooltip next to the new "Catalog Management" page.

The Decision

I chose Option 3 to keep the experience seamless and avoid user antagonism. By pairing this low-friction, contextual discovery with an email announcement detailing the feature's value, we successfully drove adoption without forcing compliance. This combined approach directly led to our high first-week completion rates.

Streamlining the Connection Flow

The core objective for this flow was speed and simplicity. To achieve this, I collaborated closely with the engineering team to identify automation opportunities and determine the absolute minimum data required from the user.

We identified a key optimization: instead of requiring users to manually input multiple campaigns, we reduced the process to a minimal input that enabled automatic account-level synchronization.

The Impact on User Experience

Massive Friction Reduction: For large-scale sellers managing many campaigns, this transformed a previously time-consuming migration into a simple, streamlined action.
Instant Feedback: After completing the connection step, users received immediate confirmation that their data was successfully synced.
Minimal input, full integration. Sellers can connect their account through a simple step that enables automatic syncing.

Edge Cases & Error Handling

Bulk Input Support: Even though the system only required a single ID, we knew sellers heavily rely on spreadsheets and might naturally copy-paste their entire list. To prevent confusion or forcing them to edit their text, the input field was designed to accept bulk IDs, handling the list seamlessly.
Partial Sync & Error States: In cases where network issues occurred or specific campaigns failed auto-detection, I designed clear error states. The system highlighted exactly which campaigns failed and provided actionable steps to resolve them without disrupting the successful syncs.

We noticed that some users missed the fact that entering just one ID would automatically sync everything. To make this crystal clear right away, I added a quick tooltip next to the input field to spell out how the automation works before they typed anything in.

Catching Unsaved Changes

Another edge case we handled was when a user adjusted their extra commission but forgot to hit "Save" before navigating away. If they tried to leave the page with unsaved changes, we triggered a modal asking if they were sure they wanted to discard them.

Since changing commission rates isn't a frequent daily task, adding this protective step didn't add any annoying friction to their regular routine–it just saved them from accidentally losing their work.

Results

The Impact

We were thrilled to see high user engagement right from the start. Due to time constraints, we launched with a closely monitored, iterative approach – tracking data and feedback from day one. My focus was translating business goals into a seamless flow, allowing us to stay agile and deploy quick fixes the moment any friction appeared.

Next steps

What's Next?

With the migration a success, we focused on optimizing the dashboard to drive deeper adoption. I sent out a seller survey to understand their daily behaviors and learn how they track performance, uncovering an amazing insight.

Sellers explicitly wanted to see a "before and after" view of how their configuration choices impacted their sales. Designing this clear ROI visualization is a massive win–it gives users the transparency they need while helping them make more informed configuration decisions, aligning user experience with business objectives.
